System Overview

A peer-to-peer LoRa mesh network mounted on coastal vessels that detects vessel distress (sinking/listing), broadcasts an alert to nearby vessels and coast guard, coordinates a helper vessel response, and relays the signal toward port when coast guard is out of direct range.

The system operates entirely over LoRa — no GSM, no internet dependency. Each device acts simultaneously as a potential distress node, helper node, and relay node depending on context. Every vessel runs the same firmware; its role is decided at runtime by a state machine, not at compile time.

Collision Avoidance Strategy

Multiple vessels may receive the same DISTRESS packet and attempt to relay or ACK simultaneously. Two mechanisms prevent collision:

1. Listen before talk (in the module): The E22 senses the channel before every transmission. The firmware contains no CAD or channel-retry code of its own.

2. Jitter delay: Before transmitting an ACK, each vessel waits a random delay proportional to its distance from the distress vessel. Closer vessels wait less, so their ACK arrives first with fewer collisions. In firmware this is a blocking delay().

Jitter Formula Jitter delay (ms) = (distance_to_distress_km / 15.0) × 2000 + random(0, 500)
Result: a vessel 3km away waits ~400–900ms; a vessel 12km away waits ~1600–2100ms.
Timing Note Airtime depends on the air rate set in the module. At the slowest LoRa settings (SF12, 125 kHz, CR 4/8) a 19-byte DISTRESS takes about 1.7 s on air, an ACK about 2 s and a RELAY about 2.5 s (standard LoRa airtime formula). ACK jitter windows and relay hops must be budgeted against that. Measure with your configured rate.

Encoding Rule All fields are binary, fixed-width. No JSON, no strings. GPS coordinates stored as int32 (value × 10⁶). All multi-byte integers are big-endian.

ROLE byte: 0x01 = Helper, 0x02 = Relayer. All vessels hear DISPATCH; non-targeted ones check TARGET_ID and ignore if not addressed to them. ROLE is a bitmask: 0x03 means one vessel is both helper and relayer. Known issue: the receiver currently requires 23 bytes, so 21-byte DISPATCH packets are rejected (see Implementation Status).

Message ID Design

MSG_ID = VESSEL_ID XOR sequence counter, 16 bits. The sequence counter increments on every transmission, so retries get fresh IDs.

This is a compact local key for the seen-cache, not a guaranteed-unique ID: different (vessel, sequence) pairs can XOR to the same value (for example vessel 1 / seq 2 and vessel 3 / seq 0). The packet carries MSG_ID and VESSEL_ID but not the sequence number itself. Recommended fix: carry VESSEL_ID and SEQ_NUM as separate header fields and key the cache on the pair.

Seen-Message Cache Each node maintains a cache of recently seen MsgIDs to avoid re-relaying or re-processing the same packet. The cache holds 32 entries (16-bit MSG_ID plus a millis() timestamp). Entries older than 10 minutes are freed, and when the cache is full the oldest entry is overwritten.

Communication Flow

Fig 6.1 — Main Distress Trigger & Broadcast Flow
As built Polling is 5 s while idle and 1.5 s once either sensor crosses its early-warning threshold. Distress triggers on tilt held at 40° or more for 2 s, on water at or above the distress level (no hold time), or on the SOS button. See section 11 for the full thresholds.

ACK & Role Assignment Logic

Fig 7.1 — ACK Collection, Role Assignment & Dispatch

Retry with backoff

If the 10 s ACK window closes with no ACKs, the node waits an idle gap and rebroadcasts with a fresh MSG_ID. The gap grows with time since the first trigger; the local alarm stays on throughout.

Time since first triggerIdle gapCycle (gap + 10 s window)
0 – 2 min0 s~10 s
2 – 10 min20 s~30 s
10 – 30 min50 s~60 s
After 30 min170 s~180 s, repeats indefinitely
Behaviour after dispatch Once at least one ACK arrives, the node sends its DISPATCH packet(s), silences the alarm and returns to idle. If the sensors are still past their thresholds, the next poll triggers a new distress. Helper and relayer vessels switch to a role state that currently has no exit and stops their own sensor polling.
08

Relay Chain Logic

Fig 8.1 — Relay Chain Toward Coast Guard
As built Forwarding is implemented: any vessel that hears a RELAY packet checks the seen-cache and the PATH for its own ID (loop), drops it at 10 hops, and otherwise increments HOP_COUNT, appends its ID (first 5 hops only) and rebroadcasts. Origination is not implemented: no code builds the first RELAY packet after a relayer is dispatched, and no coast-guard receiver is part of this firmware. The chain is dropped silently at the hop limit.

Seen-Message Cache

Every node keeps a small cache of recently seen message IDs to prevent duplicate handling and relay loops.

FieldSizePurpose
MSG_ID2 bytesVESSEL_ID XOR sequence counter. Identifies a message for the cache.

Capacity32 entriesWhen full, the oldest entry is overwritten.

Cache Rules

On receiving a DISTRESS, DISPATCH or RELAY packet: if its MSG_ID is in the cache, drop it; otherwise add it and process. A node also marks its own DISTRESS and DISPATCH transmissions as seen. ACK packets are matched against the active distress MSG_ID instead of the cache.

Eviction runs on every pass of the main loop: any entry older than 10 minutes is freed.

Edge Case The sequence counter starts at 0 on every boot and the cache is cleared at boot. A rebooted vessel can reuse MSG_IDs that other nodes still hold for up to 10 minutes, so its first distress packets may be silently dropped. Fix: seed the counter from GPS time or persistent storage, and carry VESSEL_ID and SEQ_NUM separately.

Sensing & State Estimation

The vessel state is decided by threshold rules on two independent signals, not a fused estimator. Tilt is the angle between the board z axis and gravity, atan2(sqrt(ax²+ay²), az), so pitch and roll are both captured. It uses the accelerometer only.

Placeholders Water thresholds are uncalibrated and must be set by dipping the sensor to known depths. TILT_WARNING_DEG (30°) and WATER_WARNING_RAW (1400) are defined but unused. The tilt defaults follow generic heel-angle checkpoints (30° and 40° appear in IMO righting-lever stability criteria for ships); a real vessel's downflooding angle depends on its design. The MPU6050 hardware motion interrupt is configured but not attached to an STM32 interrupt.

Known issues

1. DISPATCH length: sender transmits 21 bytes, receiver requires 23, so role assignment does not complete over the air. 2. Post-dispatch loop: the distressed node returns to idle and re-triggers if sensors are still high. 3. RELAY layout: forwarding reads HOP_COUNT at byte 19; the defined layout puts it at byte 20. 4. MSG_ID: 16-bit XOR can collide and the counter restarts at 0 on boot. 5. Blocking delays: retry gaps (up to 170 s) and ACK jitter stop radio and GPS servicing. 6. No GPS fix: zero coordinates are transmitted with no validity flag. 7. Not field-validated: range, airtime and end-to-end latency are unmeasured, and TX power of 30 dBm should be checked against current Indian regulations for the 865–867 MHz band.
